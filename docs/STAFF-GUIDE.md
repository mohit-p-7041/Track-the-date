# Track the Date — how to use it

For everyone at BP Tecoma. Print this and leave it by the counter.

It replaces the beep app. Same job: know what is going out of date, discount it before it dies,
throw out what's past. One difference worth knowing — **it records who did what**, which is what
the PIN is for. Nobody is checking up on anyone; it just means a question about an entry has an
answer.

---

## Opening up

**The laptop in the back office runs it.** Whoever opens does this once:

1. Wake the laptop up, make sure it's plugged in.
2. Double-click the green **Track the Date** icon on the desktop.
3. A black window opens with some text in it. **Leave it open.** That window is the app — closing
   it turns the app off for everybody.

That's it. Nothing to log into on the laptop, nothing to save, no shutting down at the end. If
someone closes the window by accident, double-click the icon again — nothing is lost.

## Signing in

On the iPad, tap the green **Track the Date** cup icon on the home screen.

Tap your name, type your four digits. It stays signed in for a month, so you'll rarely do this.

Not on the list, or forgotten your PIN? Anyone can add you or reset it in **Settings** — there's
no manager to wait for.

---

## The main job: adding a date

This is the thing the app exists for. It should take about five seconds an item.

1. Tap the green **+** in the bottom-right corner — it's on every screen.
2. **Scan the barcode**, or type the numbers under the stripes and press Enter.
3. **If we've seen it before**, the name comes up and it jumps straight to the date. Type the
   expiry date and tap **Save**. Done.
4. **If it's new**, it asks for the name. Type what's on the packet — however it's written. Add a
   category if you know it, leave it blank if you don't. Then the date, then **Save**.
5. It says **Saved**, and it's ready for the next one.

**Things worth knowing:**

- **"Already tracked — expires 14 Sep 2026"** means that exact item and date is already in. That's
  not an error, it's the app saving you the trouble. Move on to the next item.
- **One entry per date, not per item.** Six bottles all dying on the same day is one entry. Two
  bottles with different dates are two entries. There's no counting anywhere in this app.
- **A category is optional and always will be.** Don't let it slow you down. It attaches to the
  product, so doing it once covers every future date on that barcode.
- **Type the name as it is on the packet.** Don't tidy it up.

## The Due screen — what to do with each item

The screen you land on after signing in. Two lists:

- **Past date** — at the top, in red. These are out of date now.
- **Due within 7 days** — what's coming.

Each row has two buttons.

### Discount

Tap **Discount** when you've put a yellow sticker on it and marked it down. The row keeps a
**Discounted** tag so nobody stickers it twice.

Tapped it by mistake? **Back to full price** puts it straight back. No harm done.

### Delete

Tap **Delete** when the item is **gone from the shelf** — thrown out, sold, returned, whatever
happened to it. The row disappears for good.

- Past its date, or already discounted → it goes immediately. You're standing there holding it;
  the app doesn't need to ask.
- Still good and still full price → it asks **"this expires in 4 days, are you sure?"** first,
  because that's the one that's usually a mis-tap.

**Deleting is final.** There's no undo, and no bin to fish it out of. If you delete the wrong
thing, just scan the item again and put the date back in — that's the fix, and it takes five
seconds.

**Never delete an item just because it's been discounted.** It stays on the list, tagged, until
it actually leaves the shelf. That's how we can see what's still sitting there.

---

## The weekly sheet

**Sheet** in the menu at the top prints everything due in the next 7 days, grouped by category,
with a tick box and a blank price column.

Print it, walk the shop with it, mark your prices on it, sticker as you go. Two pages in a normal
week. Then come back and tap **Discount** on the ones you did — or do it as you go on the iPad,
whichever suits.

---

## Photos and names

Optional, and they help everyone.

Open anything from **Products**, tap **Edit**:

- **Camera** takes a photo of the item. It attaches to the product, so it shows up on every date
  for that barcode — including the ones entered months ago.
- The **name** can be fixed any time. If it came across from the old app as `C/RIDGE WATER 1L`
  and you want to make it readable, go ahead.

A photo makes the item findable on the shelf by someone who's never seen the packet before.
Worth doing for anything with a confusing name.

---

## Closing up

Close the black window on the laptop, or just shut the lid. Nothing to save — everything is
written down the moment you tap Save.

---

## If something goes wrong

**On an iPad, in this order:**

1. **Tap `Due` in the menu at the top.** That reloads the page, which is usually all it needs.
2. **Close the app fully** — swipe up and flick it away — **and tap the icon again.**
3. **Check the laptop** — is the black window still open? If not, double-click the icon.

That fixes almost everything, because almost everything is the WiFi dropping for a moment or the
laptop having been closed.

**Still not working?** Use paper for the rest of the session and tell Mohit. Nothing entered
before it broke is lost — it's all on the laptop's disk the second it's saved.

**Do not:**

- Turn the laptop off and on to "fix" it while someone else is mid-scan.
- Reinstall or update anything.
- Delete a batch of items to clear the list. The list being long is information.

## The short version

| | |
|---|---|
| Add a date | Green **+** → scan → date → Save |
| Marked it down | **Discount** |
| Wrong button | **Back to full price** |
| It's off the shelf | **Delete** |
| Already in there | Nothing to do — that message means it's handled |
| Print the round | **Sheet** |
| Broken | Refresh, reopen, check the laptop window |

Every entry has a name against it. Use your own PIN, not somebody else's — not because anyone is
policing it, but because a wrong name on an entry is worse than no name at all.
